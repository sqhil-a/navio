Program resources

Three guides for stronger career research.

Use these guides during the Career Clarity Program or on their own. Each one supports a specific program assignment.

Start the Career Clarity Program

Guide 1 · Source check

Decide whether a career claim deserves your trust.

  1. Authority: Who published it, and are they responsible for the requirement or data?
  2. Date: When was it updated? Admission, licensing, outlook, and wage information can change.
  3. Purpose: Is the page informing, recruiting, selling, or sharing one person's experience?
  4. Scope: Does it apply to Ontario, the occupation, and the pathway you are investigating?
  5. Confirmation: Can a second independent source support the claim?

Record the link, publisher, review date, and the exact fact you used. For important decisions, prefer the responsible school, regulator, government body, or official program source.

Guide 2 · Professional conversation

Ask questions that reveal the real work.

Explain that you are a student learning about the field, request a limited amount of time, and use public or appropriately supervised communication. Do not ask for a job, private mentoring, or personal contact.

  • What does a normal week include?
  • Which responsibility is least visible to students?
  • Which beginner skill matters more than people expect?
  • What tradeoff or difficult part should I understand?
  • What is one safe, low-risk way to test part of this work?

Afterward, send a short thank-you and verify factual claims such as credentials or licensing with the responsible authority.

Guide 3 · Experience test

Learn from a small action before a major commitment.

A useful test isolates one task or condition: making a simple project, observing a work process, taking a short introductory course, joining a school activity, completing a supervised volunteer task, or conducting an informational conversation.

  1. Name the question the activity should answer.
  2. Choose a scope you can complete safely.
  3. Decide what evidence you will record.
  4. Set a deadline and a reflection date.
  5. Evaluate both interest and tradeoffs afterward.

Do not treat one activity as proof that an entire career is right or wrong. Use it as evidence for the next question.
