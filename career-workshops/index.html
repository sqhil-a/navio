Career-awareness workshops

Turn a broad career interest into questions you can investigate.

A no-cost online workshop for Ontario secondary students in Grades 9–12. Learn a repeatable process for researching careers, speaking with professionals, and choosing a practical next step.

Program purpose

A practical introduction to career exploration.

The workshop is designed for students who have several interests, one early idea, or no clear direction yet. It does not ask participants to select a permanent answer. Instead, it teaches a sequence students can use whenever they need to investigate an option.

Participants learn to separate a career title from the daily work behind it, compare more than one education or training route, identify skills that can be tested now, and assess both attractive and difficult parts of a field. The goal is a better-informed next question, not a guaranteed outcome.

Learning objectives

What students will practise.

01

Define the work

Look beyond a job title to tasks, environments, responsibilities, and working relationships.

02

Compare routes

Identify education, training, experience, and alternative pathways without assuming there is only one route.

03

Ask useful questions

Prepare specific questions that reveal how a field operates and what beginners commonly misunderstand.

04

Choose a next step

Select a low-risk action such as a conversation, course, project, observation, or further research task.

Workshop agenda

From uncertainty to an exploration plan.

  1. Opening and expectations: what the workshop can and cannot provide.
  2. Career anatomy: tasks, work settings, skills, pathways, constraints, and tradeoffs.
  3. Research lab: how to evaluate sources and compare more than one route.
  4. Professional perspective: a moderated discussion focused on everyday work and lessons learned.
  5. Question practice: turning broad curiosity into specific, respectful questions.
  6. Next-step plan: one action, one person or source, and one reflection date.

Professional perspectives

Real experience, carefully framed.

Guest speakers are invited to explain their field through concrete examples: what a normal week can look like, how their route developed, which skills matter early, what surprised them, and what tradeoffs students should understand. The discussion is moderated by Navio Pathways and remains educational.

Speaker names and biographies are published only after participation and consent are confirmed. Participation does not imply an organizational partnership, endorsement, employment opportunity, or promise of individual follow-up.

Students may submit general questions during the session. Private contact between a guest and a student is not part of the workshop.

Prepare

Bring curiosity, not a final answer.

  • Choose one or two fields you are curious about, even if the interest is tentative.
  • Write down what attracts you and what you do not yet understand.
  • Bring a way to take notes and complete a short reflection.
  • Join from a space where you can listen safely and participate appropriately.

Expected outcome

Leave with work you can continue.

By the end of the session, each student should have a career-research outline, several questions for future conversations, and one realistic next action. These are learning outputs, not certifications or promises of placement, employment, admission, or volunteer-hour recognition.

Access, privacy, and safety

Designed for responsible participation.

Confirmed listings state the platform, duration, capacity, registration deadline, and any parent or guardian requirements. Registration requests only information needed to administer the session. Accessibility requests can describe useful support without including a diagnosis or medical record.

Navio uses transparent, approved communication channels. Sessions do not include unmoderated private contact between students and guest speakers. Review the privacy notice, accessibility statement, and youth-safety approach before registering.

Changes and cancellation

Frequently asked questions

Before you register.

Do I need to know which career I want?

No. The workshop is useful when you have several possibilities or are still identifying what interests you.

Is the workshop career counselling?

No. It provides general career-awareness education and does not replace individualized advice from a qualified counsellor or educator.

Will I receive volunteer hours or a certificate?

No volunteer hours or credential are promised. Any attendance acknowledgement will be described in the confirmed session listing.

How will I receive the online link?

When registration opens, accepted participants will receive access instructions at the contact email submitted.

Can I request an accommodation?

Yes. The registration workflow includes an optional space to describe the support that would help, or you can email Navio directly.
