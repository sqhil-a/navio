The competition

A real problem. A reasoned recommendation.

Teams receive the same written case about a fictional organization facing a youth, education, career, leadership, or community-access challenge. Each team identifies the central problem, weighs the people affected, compares possible responses, and develops one recommendation that can be implemented.

On competition day, teams submit a seven-slide deck and deliver a seven-minute presentation followed by five minutes of questions. Judges assess the quality of the analysis, recommendation, implementation plan, evidence, and communication.

What students practise

Analysis, teamwork, and communication.

NPCC asks students to define a problem before jumping to a solution. Teams must support their recommendation, account for tradeoffs, assign practical actions, identify risks, and answer questions under a clear time limit.

Who can enter

Ontario students in Grades 9–12.

Teams contain two to four eligible students and one adult advisor. Students may attend the same school or form a community team. Participation is online and free; no prior case-competition experience is required.
