Navio Pathways Case Competition · NPCC 2026

Build a recommendation your team can defend.

NPCC is Navio Pathways' annual online case competition for Ontario secondary students. The 2026 competition takes place Sunday, November 15, 2026.

Purpose

Turn an unfamiliar problem into a practical plan.

NPCC gives students a defined challenge, a deadline, and a common evaluation standard. The competition rewards careful problem definition, relevant evidence, feasible action, responsible tradeoffs, and clear explanation rather than a single predetermined answer.

The 2026 case concerns a fictional organization responding to a youth, education, career, leadership, or community-access problem. A fictional case lets every team work from the same information without contacting real clients or handling confidential records.

Participant outcome

Complete one team case submission.

  • A seven-slide recommendation deck
  • A seven-minute live presentation
  • A five-minute judge question period
  • A completed 100-point score sheet
  • Written panel feedback after the event

NPCC does not provide school credit, volunteer hours, employment, admission, or a cash prize.

Before Nov. 1

Team registration

Confirm eligibility and name an adult advisor.

Each team registers two to four Ontario students in Grades 9–12 and one adult advisor. The advisor may be a teacher, guidance counsellor, youth worker, parent, or guardian. The advisor receives official event communication and supports attendance and youth-safety requirements.

Action: submit the team name, student first names and grades, school or municipality, and advisor contact details.

Register for NPCC 2026
Nov. 9

Case release · 5:00 p.m. ET

Read the same case and evidence package.

Confirmed teams receive the case by email. The package defines the organization, decision, constraints, stakeholders, and evidence available to every participant. Teams may research general public context, but the recommendation must respond to the facts and limits in the supplied case.

Output: one clearly stated problem, decision criteria, and an agreed team work plan.

Nov. 9–15

Analysis and recommendation

Compare options before choosing one.

Teams identify who is affected, what success means, and which constraints cannot be ignored. They develop at least two reasonable options, compare strengths and risks, then select one recommendation with responsibilities, timing, and measures of progress.

Output: a maximum seven-slide deck, including sources and any required technology-use disclosure.

Open the preparation guide
Nov. 15

Live online competition

Present, answer questions, and receive a score.

Teams check in through the event link supplied to their advisor, submit the final deck, and present during an assigned slot. A review panel uses the published rubric. Questions test whether the team understands its evidence, tradeoffs, implementation plan, and risks.

Output: a final presentation, judge questions, and written evaluation.

100-point rubric

What judges evaluate.

  • Problem analysis — 25 points
  • Recommendation and tradeoffs — 25 points
  • Implementation and feasibility — 20 points
  • Evidence and assumptions — 15 points
  • Presentation and questions — 15 points

Registration workflow

A direct, monitored process.

The team or advisor opens the registration page, emails the required information to Navio's public organization inbox, and receives a confirmation or a specific correction request within three business days. No payment or external account is required.
