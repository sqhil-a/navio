About Navio Pathways

Practical career and leadership experience for Ontario youth.

Navio Pathways is an Ontario incorporated not-for-profit organization operating the annual Navio Pathways Case Competition for secondary students.

Mission

Help young people practise decisions that matter.

Navio Pathways creates structured opportunities for young people to investigate problems, work with others, communicate recommendations, and connect those skills to education, careers, leadership, and community contribution.

Our work is designed around active participation. Students are given a defined challenge, clear constraints, a public standard for evaluation, and an opportunity to explain their reasoning rather than only read general guidance.

Current program

Navio Pathways Case Competition

NPCC is an annual team case competition for Ontario students in Grades 9–12. The 2026 program is online, free to enter, and scheduled for November 15. Teams analyze a fictional community-focused case, submit a seven-slide recommendation, present to a review panel, and receive scored feedback.

Public accountability

Published dates, rules, and boundaries.

The competition website states eligibility, team size, registration deadline, case-release date, event schedule, deliverables, judging rubric, conduct expectations, privacy practices, and contact routes. Navio does not publish unsupported participant, partnership, sponsor, or impact claims.

How NPCC advances the mission

One experience, five transferable skills.

  • Problem definition: distinguish a cause from a symptom.
  • Evidence: connect claims to case facts and current sources.
  • Judgment: compare alternatives and accept real tradeoffs.
  • Implementation: turn an idea into owners, timing, risks, and measures.
  • Communication: present a recommendation and answer questions as a team.

Operating model

Direct delivery by Navio Pathways.

Navio publishes the competition information, accepts team registrations through its monitored organization inbox, distributes the case to confirmed advisors, administers the online event, appoints the review panel, applies the published rubric, and sends feedback.

Official communication uses the organization email and an adult team advisor. Program questions, corrections, accessibility requests, privacy requests, and youth-safety concerns all have public contact routes.

Service area

Ontario secondary students.

The current competition serves students in Grades 9–12 who live in or attend school in Ontario. Teams may represent a school or participate as a community team. Online delivery removes travel to a venue but still requires an adult advisor and compliance with event conduct and safety rules.
